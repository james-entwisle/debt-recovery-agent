2. Our initial strategic interpretation

InDebted’s existing product and opportunity

InDebted has an AI capability called Composer.

Composer generates personalised collection emails and SMS using consumer account information, payment history and previous engagement.

A separate model reviews generated content for potential compliance issues.

Composer is already live in Australia, Canada and the UAE, where personalised messages are evaluated against templated alternatives.

However, Composer currently operates within a fixed collections contact schedule.

It determines what a scheduled message might say, but not the broader logic of when the journey should change in response to consumer behaviour.

Composer is not yet authorised for US production use. The US is described as InDebted’s largest market and currently uses templated communications.

The current example schedule is:

Journey day	Channel	Purpose
0	Email	Account and options
3	SMS	Reminder
7	Email	Payment and help options
14	SMS	Reminder
21	Email	Options and assistance

The schedule already responds to certain account states, such as pending payments, confirmed plans and account holds.

However, it does not adapt to engagement itself.

A message click, authenticated portal visit or incomplete plan does not change the sequence.

This creates an opportunity to make the collections journey itself adaptive, rather than focusing exclusively on generating better message copy.

Working investment thesis

Our working product concept is:

Adaptive Resolution

A consumer-intent-aware collections journey that responds to engagement events, provides relevant guidance in the portal and dynamically coordinates future communications.

The opportunity is not simply to generate more messages.

It is to decide the most appropriate next consumer interaction based on behaviour, authoritative account state, demonstrated intent and consumer preferences.

The system should sometimes:

* Present a more relevant portal experience.
* Replace a generic scheduled message with an approved contextual alternative.
* Defer contact until a permitted time.
* Route a consumer to appropriate assistance.
* Suppress contact entirely.
* Take no additional action.

The long-term vision is an intelligent journey orchestration capability that works alongside Composer and the existing account, contact and portal systems.

The immediate investment is a bounded experiment to determine whether such a capability produces enough additional settled collections to justify further development.

This is our working thesis, not a predetermined conclusion. Challenge it when the evidence warrants doing so.

3. How we arrived at this direction

Capture this reasoning in a concise docs/discovery.md or equivalent working document.

We considered several opportunities before arriving at Adaptive Resolution.

Option A: Roll out Composer in the US

Opportunity:

* Broad potential reach.
* Existing technology and messaging infrastructure.
* Improved contextual relevance of message content.

Limitations:

* No US production approval.
* No approved US prompt set or evaluation results.
* No causal evidence of additional collections.
* Better message wording does not fundamentally address the rigid schedule.

Conclusion: Not our preferred first investment.

Option B: Send reminders after abandoned repayment plans

Opportunity:

* High-intent segment.
* Clear observable event.
* Direct supporting interviews.
* Straightforward to implement.

Limitations:

* Only 2,000 consumers per synthetic monthly intake.
* Many consumers pay anyway under the current experience.
* A reminder may not solve the underlying reason a consumer left.
* A narrow investment has limited commercial upside.

Conclusion: Valuable initial use case, but too narrow as the full investment thesis.

Option C: Improve repayment-plan building

Opportunity:

* Consumer interviews identify payment-date mismatch, interruptions and uncertainty.
* Could improve consumer outcomes and plan completion.
* Creates a pathway towards affordability-aware repayment experiences.

Limitations:

* Existing creditor-approved terms cannot be modified in the four-week pilot.
* A new interface cannot create financial flexibility that the creditor has not authorised.
* Portal-only improvements do not address later re-engagement and the existing contact schedule.

Conclusion: A valuable component, but not a complete response to the problem described in the brief.

Option D: Activate consumers with low or no engagement

Opportunity:

* Much larger population.
* Small changes in paid conversion could create considerable incremental contribution.

Limitations:

* Weak qualitative evidence explaining their behaviour.
* Cannot assume non-engagement reflects a communication problem.
* May require different interventions or additional discovery.

Conclusion: Attractive future expansion, but more uncertain as the first test.

Recommended direction: Adaptive Resolution

Combine the strongest elements of the options above:

1. Capture consumer intent through a small, adaptive portal experience.
2. Respond to observable engagement events.
3. Coordinate contextual follow-ups with the existing schedule.
4. Use approved repayment options and safe assistance routes.
5. Validate the incremental commercial outcome.
6. Expand to additional journey states only when justified.

This offers more meaningful product capability than an abandoned-plan reminder, without requiring a full collections-platform rebuild.

Keep this alternatives assessment visible but concise.

The final decision note should demonstrate that we considered alternatives, selected an investment and understood its opportunity cost.

4. Consumer problem and target segment

Consumer problem

Consumers can demonstrate interest in resolving their debt without being ready or able to make an immediate commitment.

The current journey does not sufficiently respond to their behaviour or help distinguish between different barriers to resolution.

A generic scheduled reminder may arrive despite the consumer having already visited the portal, reviewed repayment options or requested help.

A consumer who starts but does not confirm a repayment plan may need:

* An arrangement aligned with their pay cycle.
* Clarification of the balance or creditor.
* Time to review household expenses.
* Assistance finding an appropriate option.
* An opportunity to return after an interruption.

The problem is not necessarily a lack of willingness to pay.

Our hypothesis is that timely, relevant and consumer-led next steps will help more consumers progress to sustainable repayment.

Target segment

Target US consumers with an observed engagement signal but no active repayment plan or pending payment at the initial decision point.

The initial opportunity comprises three mutually exclusive historical cohorts:

Cohort	Consumers per 30-day intake	Paid within 30 days	Settled cash within 30 days
Message click only	10,000	18%	$306,000
Portal visit, no plan started	6,000	30%	$324,000
Plan started, incomplete	2,000	50%	$190,000
Combined	18,000	25.6%	$820,000

All figures are synthetic USD amounts.

The cohorts are mutually exclusive at t0, not sequential steps in a measured funnel.

Do not depict their population sizes or conversion rates as if they were observed transitions between stages.

A consumer’s cohort reflects the deepest engagement signal in the 24 hours before t0.

All included consumers have no active plan or pending payment at t0, but their state may change later.

Why these cohorts?

They provide an observable basis for contextual intervention.

The deeper the engagement, the more we may know about the consumer’s next useful action.

However, greater engagement is correlated with greater payment conversion. We do not know whether engagement causes payment.

Importantly, the eight interviews only cover consumers who left an incomplete plan.

Do not assume their reasons generalise to the entire 18,000-consumer population.

Pilot population versus expansion opportunity

Distinguish:

Addressable opportunity: The 18,000 engaged consumers in the three historical cohorts.

Initial implementation: A small set of event-driven journeys, particularly authenticated portal visits and incomplete plan starts, with message clicks used as a weaker engagement signal.

Effective reach: The subset of consumers who actually encounter the intervention and remain eligible.

Do not use the entire 18,000-person opportunity to justify the investment without modelling intervention reach and its uncertainty.

A click alone is not evidence that someone successfully authenticated or reached the portal.

5. Evidence from consumer research

The supporting pack includes eight interviews with consumers who started but did not complete a repayment plan.

Findings:

Observation	Interviews
Available payment dates didn’t align with payday	3/8
Consumer was interrupted and expected to return	2/8
Creditor or balance was unclear	2/8
Concern about committing before reviewing expenses	1/8

The operational observations also include 50 support conversations after portal visits:

* 12 about plan dates.
* 9 about account or balance questions.
* 7 about resuming an unfinished plan.
* 22 about other topics.

These observations suggest that payment-date suitability, information clarity and interrupted journeys are meaningful problems.

They do not establish population-level prevalence or causal effects.

Crucial product nuance

plan_started does not mean a repayment plan was confirmed.

Consumers have entered the plan flow but have not explicitly committed to an arrangement.

We do not know which stage they reached or why they left.

Existing plans cannot be saved and resumed.

The existing event stream records that a plan flow started, but not the reason for non-completion.

Consequently:

* Do not invent consumer motivations.
* Treat an incomplete plan as a behavioural signal, not proof of willingness or ability to pay.
* Collect explicit preferences where useful.
* Distinguish stated intent from inferred intent.
* Do not claim the initial intervention can resume saved plan progress when the underlying capability does not exist.

Our system should improve both relevance and the information available for future product decisions.

6. Product hypothesis and commercial hypothesis

Product hypothesis

For engaged US consumers without an active arrangement, an adaptive journey that captures consumer needs, provides a relevant portal experience and adjusts subsequent communications will increase useful consumer actions and ultimately settled payments compared with the existing fixed journey.

Mechanisms to test:

1. More relevant portal interactions help consumers understand their options and identify the next useful action.
2. Capturing explicit preferences reduces ambiguity about why consumers do not progress.
3. Contextual follow-ups can make the next scheduled communication more useful.
4. Better orchestration can avoid unnecessary or inappropriate contacts.
5. Connecting the interaction across multiple touchpoints improves the likelihood of resolution.

These mechanisms are hypotheses, not established facts.

The initial randomised experiment tests the combined journey, rather than isolating the independent effect of the portal and the messaging changes.

Commercial hypothesis

A relatively small absolute improvement in payment conversion across a sufficiently large engaged population could generate meaningful incremental settled cash and retained collections contribution.

We need to establish whether that contribution justifies the initial investment and subsequent expansion.

The primary commercial outcome is incremental settled cash, not message responses, clicks, portal visits or plan confirmations.

InDebted earns its commission when payments settle.

7. Commercial modelling requirements

Implement a small, transparent financial model.

This can be a reusable calculation in the application, a lightweight spreadsheet or a clearly documented analysis script.

Ideally, expose the assumptions through editable controls in the internal demo.

Supplied economics

All figures in USD.

* Retained collections fee: 15% of settled cash.
* Variable payment cost: 2% of settled cash.
* Email cost: $0.003 per send.
* SMS cost: $0.035 per send.
* Composer generation/review attempt: $0.012.
* Incremental support conversation: $6.
* Engineering capacity: 40 engineer-days at $1,000 per day.
* Shared design/compliance allocation: $10,000.
* Maximum four-week allocation: $50,000.

The baseline marginal contribution from incremental collections before additional communication and support costs is therefore 13%.

For example:

$100 incremental settled cash
→ $15 retained revenue
→ $2 variable payment cost
→ $13 contribution before incremental messaging and support.

Do not confuse:

* Total recovered cash.
* InDebted retained revenue.
* Marginal contribution.
* Net investment return.

Do not count the full collected debt as company revenue.

Historical baseline

All five cohorts combined:

* 100,000 consumers per 30-day intake.
* $1.78m settled cash.
* $231,400 contribution at 13%, before communication and support costs.

The 18,000-person engaged opportunity:

* $820,000 settled cash per 30-day intake.
* Approximately 25.6% 30-day paid conversion.
* Approximately $106,600 contribution at 13%, before other variable costs.

These are baseline economics, not incremental investment value.

Conversion lift model

Use the historical average cash collected per paying consumer as an illustrative proxy:

* Clicked-only cohort: $170 per paying consumer.
* Portal-only cohort: $180 per paying consumer.
* Incomplete-plan cohort: $190 per paying consumer.

At full reach, each absolute one-percentage-point improvement across all three cohorts represents approximately $31,600 in additional settled cash per 30-day intake.

A three-percentage-point improvement therefore implies:

* $94,800 additional monthly settled cash.
* $14,220 additional retained revenue.
* $1,896 payment costs.
* $12,324 monthly contribution before incremental messaging/support.
* $147,888 annualised contribution before incremental messaging/support.

This is a scenario, not a forecast.

A ten-point improvement would approach $493k in annual contribution before other variable costs, but this is an optimistic upside case rather than a defensible base prediction.

Reach-adjusted modelling

Build an interactive model with these assumptions:

* Monthly population.
* Cohort mix.
* Eligible and effectively reached percentage.
* Absolute paid-conversion improvement among reached consumers.
* Average settled cash per additional payer.
* Incremental communication costs.
* Additional support conversation rate.
* Investment cost.
* Persistence of lift across successive monthly cohorts.

Suggested scenarios:

* Conservative: +1 percentage point.
* Base: +3 percentage points.
* Upside: +5 percentage points.
* Stretch: +10 percentage points, clearly labelled speculative.

Example base assumptions:

* 60% effective reach.
* +3 percentage-point paid-conversion improvement among reached consumers.
* 2% incremental support conversation rate among reached consumers.
* $50,000 one-off investment.

These imply approximately $73k annual incremental contribution after additional support cost, but before other possible incremental costs.

Show simple payback.

Allow assumptions to be changed without modifying formulas.

Do not assume a contextual message improves conversion unless the pilot establishes it.

Important commercial nuance

A newly converted payer might settle less than the historical average payer.

The historical average is a modelling proxy, not an observed incremental payment amount.

Plan confirmations have no direct retained collections fee until instalments settle.

Longer-term plan adherence and 60/90-day cash outcomes are unknown.

Distinguish acceleration of payments from genuinely incremental payments.

Investment decision

Recommend a four-week pilot with a maximum $50,000 allocation, but recognise that a smaller initial build may be justified.

A useful investment decision should evaluate:

* Expected incremental annual contribution.
* Simple payback.
* Safety and consumer outcomes.
* Technical and compliance complexity.
* Opportunity cost.
* Whether the initial effect can credibly persist or scale.
* Whether additional evidence is required before expansion.

Do not make the model artificially optimistic to justify the decision.

8. The actual product we’re building

Build a working end-to-end demonstration of Adaptive Resolution.

The demonstration should contain two connected experiences:

1. An adaptive consumer portal.
2. An internal contextual journey orchestration simulator.

Both should operate from the same underlying consumer state and decision rules.

The goal is to demonstrate product value and technical substance, not merely design aesthetics.

A. Adaptive consumer portal

Use Next.js and HeroUI, with a clean, restrained financial-services design.

The portal represents an authenticated consumer experience.

Show an illustrative creditor, account balance and available actions using synthetic account data.

Do not bypass or simulate insecure authentication as if it were production-ready.

The consumer should encounter a clear prompt such as:

“Let’s find your next step.”

Depending on their context, offer appropriate choices:

* Make a one-off payment.
* Explore repayment options.
* Find an arrangement that fits payday.
* Understand the creditor or balance.
* Request help.
* Review options later without making a commitment.

The UI should be adaptive, but not a generic chatbot.

Payment preference interaction

If a consumer wants a suitable repayment arrangement, collect optional preferences such as:

* Next payday.
* Payment frequency: weekly, fortnightly or monthly.
* Preferred instalment amount or affordability concern.

Use these inputs to match against existing creditor-approved repayment plans.

Do not fabricate new approved terms.

Where the supplied scenario data lacks plan options, create explicitly labelled synthetic fixtures and document them separately from supplied data.

Implement a transparent matching function that can explain why options match or do not match.

If no approved option fits:

* Say so clearly.
* Do not invent a new arrangement.
* Offer to submit a structured assistance request.
* Do not promise immediate human support.

A consumer must explicitly confirm an approved plan before it becomes active.

For the prototype, plan confirmation and payments may be simulated, but the behaviour must be clear.

Consumer intent

Record structured intent such as:

* explore_payment
* explore_plan
* payday_preference
* balance_question
* affordability_concern
* request_help
* review_later

These should be explicitly selected by the consumer rather than inferred from arbitrary engagement signals.

Keep intent data use and retention considerations visible in the documentation.

Contextual arrival

If a consumer returns to the portal following a relevant event or follow-up, display an appropriate next step rather than assuming every consumer wants to pay immediately.

This can be a small contextual landing interaction after authentication.

Do not claim to resume a previously saved incomplete plan unless we have implemented a simulated save/resume capability and clearly labelled it as future functionality.

B. Contextual journey orchestration

The second view should demonstrate the underlying decision-making system.

It must show:

* Consumer identity and account state.
* Recent engagement events.
* Existing scheduled communications.
* Recorded consumer intent, where available.
* Contact permissions.
* Applicable policy constraints.
* The decision taken.
* The reason for that decision.
* Any proposed scheduled action.
* What changes compared with the original fixed schedule.

Use a timeline or decision replay interface.

Behavioural trigger families

Support these event types, with different confidence levels:

1. Message click

The consumer has clicked a link but may not have authenticated or entered the portal.

This is a relatively weak signal.

The system may consider a contextual replacement for a later eligible scheduled message, but should not invent a deeper consumer motivation.

2. Authenticated portal visit

The consumer reached the account portal but did not progress to an active plan or pending payment.

The system can determine whether subsequent contact should be adapted or whether a portal interaction can provide a useful next step.

A visit alone is not consent to additional messages.

3. Plan started but not confirmed

The consumer entered the plan-building flow but has not committed to an arrangement.

After a defined waiting interval, the system can evaluate whether a contextual follow-up would be useful.

It must not automatically assume they want to proceed.

4. Explicit consumer preference

The consumer indicates a specific need, such as payday alignment, balance clarification or assistance.

This can influence the portal experience and appropriately approved follow-up content.

It does not override creditor terms or communication permissions.

5. Account or preference change

A payment is initiated, a plan becomes active, a consumer opts out, or an account hold appears.

The system re-evaluates its proposed next action and suppresses or changes behaviour when required.

Candidate decisions

Design an explicit set of outcomes:

* ADAPT_PORTAL
* REPLACE_CONTACT
* RESCHEDULE
* DEFER
* SUPPRESS
* NO_ACTION
* ROUTE_TO_SUPPORT

Not every event should result in a new communication.

For the initial pilot, prefer replacing or coordinating an existing scheduled contact instead of adding more contacts.

Demonstrate how the original schedule differs from the proposed journey.

A user should be able to understand:

* What event was observed?
* What did the system know?
* What did it decide?
* Why?
* What would the consumer experience?
* When would that happen?
* What would happen if their circumstances changed?

10. Safety and compliance requirements

The pilot rules in the supporting pack are mandatory.

Implement the relevant rules as deterministic safeguards, not as suggestions from a language model.

For outbound pilot messages:

* Require pilot_scope_allowed=true.
* Require language=en.
* Respect channel-specific permissions.
* Suppress contact if the balance is zero, payment is pending, a plan is active, or the account has a hold.
* Treat unknown required eligibility values as ineligible for outreach.
* Respect verified timezone and local contact windows.
* Send pilot reminders only Monday to Friday, 9 am inclusive to 6 pm exclusive, unless a narrower permitted window applies.
* Enforce one non-transactional outbound attempt per rolling 24 hours.
* Enforce three non-transactional outbound attempts per rolling seven days.
* Count attempts across all channels.
* Include boundary-time attempts according to the supplied rules.
* Count provider-handoff attempts even if delivery fails.
* Coordinate triggered and scheduled contacts to avoid double-sending.
* Re-read authoritative state before sending.
* Handle duplicate, delayed and out-of-order events.
* Log decisions, reason codes, template versions, inputs and outcomes.
* Provide a simulated stop control for the pilot.

Do not assume an open, click or portal visit grants communication consent.

Use authoritative account information for balances and available terms.

No model may:

* Determine legal eligibility.
* Invent or change repayment terms.
* Create an agreement without explicit consumer confirmation.
* Authorise a debit.
* Invent consequences, deadlines, discounts or promises.
* Override deterministic safety rules.

Keep the fixed, versioned disclosure and opt-out component in outbound message previews, identified as a placeholder.

Do not invent legal disclosure language.

A human compliance reviewer must approve material journey changes and outbound templates before any real pilot.

For held, disputed, hardship or otherwise restricted accounts, make the safe outcome visible without inventing payment pressure.

Compliance is part of the product behaviour, not merely a paragraph in the decision note.

11. Composer and AI behaviour

Composer is relevant to the strategic narrative, but a US production rollout is not required or authorised.

For the initial implementation:

* Use approved template selection.
* Optionally demonstrate mock Composer drafting in a clearly labelled sandbox.
* Show the distinction between deciding the next action and composing its content.
* Keep eligibility and permissions deterministic.
* Never treat model-based review as legal approval.

Our conceptual architecture has two independent responsibilities:

Journey intelligence: Whether to act, what action to take and when.

Content intelligence: What approved content to present after an action has been determined.

If useful, show a future interface through which Composer could draft an approved contextual message.

Do not spend the majority of the build implementing a language-model integration.

Evaluations

Create two kinds of evaluations.

Deterministic safety and correctness evaluations

Do decisions comply with the supplied policy and current state?

Examples:

* Opt-out suppresses a queued send.
* Pending payment suppresses a queued send.
* Unknown timezone prevents pilot outreach.
* Hardship hold prevents outreach.
* Duplicate events do not create duplicate actions.
* Cross-channel contact limits are respected.
* A plan is never created without explicit confirmation.

Message and product-quality evaluations

If AI-generated content is demonstrated in the sandbox:

* Is the content grounded in verified account information?
* Does it avoid invented repayment terms?
* Does it preserve autonomy?
* Does it avoid inappropriate pressure?
* Does it send consumers towards the correct approved next step?

These evaluations are not the commercial outcome.

The commercial outcome is measured through the randomised pilot.

14. Four-week pilot

Design a limited experiment comparing the new adaptive journey with the existing fixed experience.

Experimental design

Randomise eligible consumers at the appropriate pre-treatment decision point.

Control

* Existing portal experience.
* Existing fixed communication schedule.
* Existing approved repayment options.

Treatment

* Adaptive portal next-step experience.
* Contextual journey orchestration.
* Approved contextual message templates.
* Same underlying creditor-approved terms.

Assign experiments consistently per consumer.
