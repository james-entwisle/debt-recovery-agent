# Adaptive Resolution V2: Conversational Agent and Just-in-Time Resolution UI

## Your mission

We are pivoting the product experience for the InDebted Product Lead case study.

**Preserve our existing Adaptive Resolution investment thesis, core decision engine, compliance rules, cohort analysis and commercial model. Replace the confusing journey simulator with a compelling two-way SMS conversation simulator, and reposition the portal as a set of just-in-time (JIT) interfaces surfaced when consumers need to complete specific actions.**

This is an evolution of our existing implementation, not a greenfield rebuild.

We want to demonstrate what a competent, persistent AI-powered debt-resolution assistant could look like if InDebted evolved Composer from a one-way outbound message generator into a two-way, context-aware consumer-resolution experience.

The product should feel like an ongoing conversation with an assistant that understands the consumer's needs, remembers relevant context and helps them progress towards appropriate voluntary resolution.

It must not resemble an aggressive autonomous debt collector.

**Inspect the current repository before modifying anything. Reuse existing working code and infrastructure wherever possible.**

We are under a strict three-hour take-home timebox. Implement a coherent working vertical slice quickly.

---

# 1. Revised product thesis

## Current state

InDebted's Composer personalises outbound collection messages but operates within a fixed schedule.

It doesn't currently respond to consumer engagement events such as clicks, authenticated portal visits or incomplete repayment-plan flows.

The supporting pack also describes a portal where consumers can review accounts, make payments, choose existing approved plans and ask for help.

However:

- Existing links open the generic account landing page after authentication.
- The journey is not responsive to consumer engagement.
- Incomplete repayment plans cannot be saved and resumed.
- The event stream records a plan start but not why someone leaves.
- The US market uses templated messages, and Composer lacks US production approval.

The pack does not establish that inbound SMS conversations, magic-link authentication or persistent conversational sessions already exist.

Treat those as proposed product capabilities.

## Revised opportunity

**Transform collections from a series of scheduled one-way communications into an ongoing, contextual resolution conversation.**

A consumer should be able to reply to a collections SMS with something natural, such as:

"I want to pay but I don't get paid until next Friday."

The assistant should understand the expressed need, identify what it can safely help with, and offer a meaningful next action.

For example:

- Capture a payday preference.
- Explain the options available for the authenticated account.
- Surface a focused payment-plan interface.
- Save an unfinished draft for later where permitted.
- Offer a secure return link.
- Route an account question or affordability concern to support.
- Reconsider subsequent contact based on current account state and prior interactions.

The assistant should not force the consumer through a comprehensive portal experience unless they specifically want to access it.

## Product hypothesis

For engaged US consumers without an active repayment arrangement, a two-way contextual assistant with task-specific resolution interfaces will improve consumers' ability to progress and increase incremental settled collections compared with the existing fixed journey.

## Commercial hypothesis

Retain our existing 18,000-consumer engaged opportunity:

| Cohort | Consumers | 30-day settled cash |
|---|---:|---:|
| Click only | 10,000 | $306,000 |
| Authenticated portal visit | 6,000 | $324,000 |
| Incomplete plan | 2,000 | $190,000 |
| Total | 18,000 | $820,000 |

Our earlier full-reach three-percentage-point uplift scenario represented approximately $148k annual contribution before new communications and support costs.

This remains a useful sensitivity scenario, not a forecast.

The conversational model introduces additional assumptions around SMS availability, response rate, AI interaction costs, escalations and conversion among responders.

Do not change the commercial baseline unnecessarily. Extend the existing model to reflect the new delivery mechanism.

# 3. Tab 1: SMS Simulator

## Objective

Demonstrate a realistic two-way SMS conversation between an InDebted resolution assistant and a consumer, driven by account state, conversational intent and existing journey orchestration.

This should be the centrepiece of the interview demonstration.

## Layout

Use a three-column desktop layout.

### Left: Consumer and scenario controls

Display a compact consumer-state inspector:

- Selected scenario.
- Consumer/account identifier.
- Current balance.
- Payment status.
- Plan status.
- Pilot scope.
- Channel permissions.
- Account holds.
- Consumer timezone.
- Existing queued contact.
- Most recent detected intent.
- Conversation/session status.

Provide a scenario selector using the supplied synthetic scenarios.

Include state controls that allow us to simulate changes, for example:

- Payment becomes pending.
- Payment settles.
- Plan becomes active.
- Consumer opts out.
- Hardship or dispute hold appears.
- Consumer becomes ineligible for contact.
- Contact window/budget is exhausted.

State changes should trigger actual re-evaluation by the decision engine.

Do not merely swap hard-coded visual outcomes.

Add a reset action that restores the selected scenario to its original fixture state.

### Centre: SMS conversation

Create a credible phone-style SMS interface with:

- InDebted assistant identity.
- Chat bubbles for inbound and outbound messages.
- Message timestamps.
- Message status, clearly identifying simulated sending.
- Scrollable conversation history.
- Text input for custom consumer replies.
- Send button.
- Typing/loading state where useful.
- Suggested example consumer responses for fast demonstration.
- Secure links or contextual action cards when the assistant proposes an authenticated task.
- Fixed disclosure/opt-out component placeholders where required.

The conversation must be interactive.

A consumer message should go through a real intent-processing and next-action flow, not simply append text or select a hard-coded response.

Example inbound messages:

"I want to pay but I don't get paid until next Friday."

"Can I pay $50 every fortnight?"

"Who is this creditor?"

"I don't recognise this debt."

"I'm struggling with money at the moment."

"I started setting up a plan yesterday but got interrupted."

"I've already paid."

"STOP"

Each should produce a demonstrably different outcome.

Use short, credible and non-coercive responses.

Do not place debt details in unverified message previews.

The message history should clearly distinguish:

- Previously scheduled outbound message.
- Inbound consumer response.
- Assistant's proposed reply.
- Approved simulated reply.
- Suppressed or blocked reply where relevant.

### Right: Agent decisions and next action

Show the intelligence behind the conversation without making it the primary visual experience.

Include:

- Detected intent.
- Relevant consumer context.
- Authoritative account checks.
- Allowed actions.
- Chosen next action.
- Decision reasoning.
- Approved template or response reference.
- Escalation requirement.
- Future scheduled contact changes.
- Just-in-time portal action, if applicable.

For example:

**Detected intent:** Payday alignment  
**Account:** Eligible  
**Known preference:** Fortnightly income  
**Next action:** Review approved arrangements  
**UI:** Secure plan-matching interface  
**Outbound schedule:** Existing queued contact retained or replaced according to policy

The right panel should update as the conversation progresses.

Keep the audit trace expandable rather than overwhelming the page.

## Scenario presets

Provide at least these demonstration scenarios:

### A. Payday alignment

Consumer has an outstanding account and no active repayment plan.

They respond to outreach:

"I get paid fortnightly. Can I start after next Friday?"

The assistant:

1. Recognises an explicit payday preference.
2. Captures the declared timing and frequency.
3. Offers to review existing creditor-approved arrangements.
4. Opens a focused, authenticated plan-matching interface.
5. If no arrangement fits, offers a structured help request.
6. Does not invent a new date or promise approval.

### B. Interrupted plan

Consumer started but did not confirm a repayment arrangement.

They ask to continue where they left off.

Demonstrate the distinction between:

- Today's unsupported save/resume behaviour.
- The proposed resumable draft capability.

If a draft was saved in the demonstration, offer a secure return link and restore that draft after authentication.

If no draft exists, explain that the consumer must start a new selection and provide a useful entry point.

Do not imply a past `plan_started` event contains saved form data.

### C. Account question or dispute

Consumer asks who the creditor is or questions their balance.

The assistant should offer verified account information through a secure authenticated surface or route the issue to support.

For a dispute, avoid continuing payment persuasion and demonstrate the appropriate hold/escalation route.

Do not let the model arbitrarily set authoritative account holds. Use the source-state workflow and explicitly simulate any hold that a human or account service would apply.

### D. Payment initiated

Using supplied S06 or equivalent, the consumer initiates payment after the assistant has proposed a follow-up.

The authoritative account state updates before the delayed event arrives.

The system should recheck state and suppress inappropriate follow-up.

### E. Opt-out

Using supplied S05 or equivalent, the consumer opts out after an action has been queued.

The source-state update immediately prevents later promotional/collections outreach despite delayed event delivery.

### F. Hardship

Consumer indicates financial hardship.

Show appropriate acknowledgement and assistance routing, with no pressure to commit.

Any formal hardship hold must be represented as an authoritative state change, not an unsupported model decision.

## Original scenario compatibility

Preserve the supplied S01 to S12 fixtures and relevant regression tests.

The supplied scenarios are about contact/event decisions, not fully populated conversational histories.

Extend them using separate, clearly labelled synthetic inbound SMS fixtures.

Do not mutate the original files or claim the invented messages came from InDebted's dataset.

# 4. Two-way conversational agent

## Core flow

Implement a demonstrable pipeline:

1. Receive simulated inbound SMS.
2. Associate it with the correct conversation and consumer.
3. Identify explicit intent using a deterministic classifier or optional AI sandbox.
4. Retrieve relevant permitted context.
5. Evaluate available next actions.
6. Apply policy and account-state constraints.
7. Select an approved response or proposed escalation.
8. Update conversation state and scheduled actions.
9. Generate a secure JIT interface link where appropriate.
10. Log the decision and outcome.

The conversation should retain useful context across turns.

Example:

Consumer: "I get paid every second Friday."

Assistant: "Would you like to review repayment options that might fit your pay cycle?"

Consumer: "Yes, around $50."

The assistant should remember the fortnightly timing and evaluate the amount preference without asking the consumer to repeat everything.

Conversation state should include:

- Session ID.
- Consumer/account ID.
- Conversation history.
- Last detected intent.
- Explicit consumer preferences.
- Proposed next action.
- Related JIT action.
- Current account status reference.
- Last evaluated contact decision.
- Simulated timestamps.

## Optional LLM mode

Add a toggle:

- **Deterministic simulation**
- **AI sandbox**, if feasible

The deterministic mode must work fully without any API key or external network dependency.

If an AI sandbox is implemented, allow an optional key through a server-side development configuration or session-scoped secure input.

Never embed an API key in frontend source, commit it, persist it in local storage, or include it in logs.

The model should only interpret intent and propose response content using permitted context.

Do not give the model autonomous authority to:

- Approve repayment terms.
- Confirm plans.
- Authorise payments.
- Decide communication eligibility.
- Override contact budgets.
- Determine legal outcomes.
- Release account holds.

If a model proposes something unsupported, the policy/action layer must reject it.

Do not make real provider integrations a dependency for the demonstration.

## Response quality

Create a handful of explicit evaluations:

- Correct intent extraction.
- Correct handling of multi-turn payday preferences.
- No invented approved repayment terms.
- Appropriate assistance for questions and hardship.
- Opt-out handling.
- Correct suppression after a pending payment.
- No assumed permission based on inbound engagement.
- Appropriate human escalation.

For the initial US pilot proposal, treat generated outbound content as sandbox-only unless a human-approved production pathway exists.

All new material journey or message behaviours require compliance review.

# 6. Session management and resumable plan drafts

This is an important proposed improvement to the existing experience.

The supporting pack says unfinished plans cannot currently be saved and resumed, meaning a consumer who leaves the flow has to start over.

We want to demonstrate a solution, while clearly identifying it as new functionality that would need to be built.

## Session model

Implement a lightweight session-management system supporting:

- A persistent simulated conversation session.
- A consumer identity/account association.
- A JIT action reference.
- A saved repayment-plan draft.
- Last updated timestamp.
- Session status.
- Session expiry.
- Explicit confirmed versus unconfirmed plan status.

Prefer server-side state. Avoid storing account details or sensitive draft data in browser local storage.

For the demo, a lightweight repository or SQLite store is acceptable if one is already available or easy to add. Otherwise use a clean session abstraction with a clearly disclosed persistence limitation.

## Saving plan progress

A consumer should be able to:

1. Open approved repayment options.
2. Select preferences or an existing approved arrangement.
3. Start completing the arrangement.
4. Save their draft without committing.
5. Leave the flow.
6. Return through a secure simulated link.
7. Authenticate.
8. Resume the draft.
9. Revalidate the approved terms and account state.
10. Explicitly confirm the arrangement.

A saved draft is not an active plan or repayment agreement.

Saving or resuming a draft must never automatically create a repayment obligation or trigger a debit.

If the account state or creditor-approved options have changed, the system must invalidate or re-evaluate the draft rather than blindly restore outdated terms.

If a draft has expired, show an appropriate recovery experience.

Add an explicit "Save and return later" action if technically feasible.

## Implementation priority

Demonstrate at least one real save/leave/resume interaction with server-side state if feasible within the existing architecture.

If full draft persistence threatens the core demonstration, implement the session contract and a bounded prototype, document the remaining production dependency and keep the main SMS and JIT flows working.

Do not build a complete production-grade payment-plan persistence system under the take-home timebox.

# 7. Magic-link authentication and low-friction return

This should reinforce the product thesis: a consumer should not have to repeatedly navigate the account portal or re-enter information just to continue a useful interaction.

However, the case only establishes authentication before account access. It does not establish an existing magic-link mechanism.

Treat magic links as a **proposed new authentication capability**.

## Desired experience

SMS conversation:

"I can help you review your available arrangements."

Consumer selects the secure action link.

They authenticate through a short-lived, purpose-bound link or a similarly low-friction approved mechanism.

They reach the relevant JIT interface.

If the link refers to a saved draft, the draft is restored after identity and account association are verified.

## Demo implementation

Implement a simulated magic-link flow that is meaningfully more than an unconditional "authenticated=true" flag.

Where practical:

- Generate a cryptographically random opaque token.
- Associate the token server-side with a consumer/account and action.
- Give it a short expiry.
- Make it single-use.
- Validate it server-side.
- Exchange it for an appropriately scoped authenticated demo session.
- Use an HTTP-only session cookie.
- Prevent a token from granting access to unrelated accounts or actions.
- Reject expired or reused links.
- Never log raw tokens.
- Avoid exposing account-sensitive information before successful verification.

Show the generated link inside the local simulator.

Do not send real SMS or emails.

Add a way to test expired links and invalid sessions.

Label this clearly as **simulated authentication infrastructure, not production-ready identity verification**.

Production authentication would require a separate security review covering identity verification, phone-number reassignment, shared devices, link leakage, throttling, abuse prevention, auditability and privacy.

A link delivered to a phone number is not, by itself, sufficient proof of identity.

## Friction reduction

Use session state to avoid asking the consumer to repeat already captured non-sensitive preferences.

Where appropriate, preserve a saved draft across authenticated sessions.

Require explicit review and confirmation before any plan or payment is committed.

This creates a direct answer to the interview finding that some consumers were interrupted and couldn't resume.

# 8. Decision orchestration and existing schedule

Preserve our existing Elixir/Ash decision engine.

Do not rewrite a functioning implementation unnecessarily.

Extend it to accept conversational intent and JIT outcomes as additional context.

## Important distinction

There are two different classes of action:

**Consumer-initiated service interaction**

A consumer messages the assistant, asks a question or opens a secure interface.

**Proactive collections outreach**

InDebted decides to initiate another communication.

Do not treat them as interchangeable.

The supporting pack excludes requested service replies from the example scheduled contact budget, but that does not constitute unrestricted authorisation for arbitrary outbound conversation.

Implement separate action classifications and document the assumptions requiring compliance confirmation.

## Scheduled outreach

The existing schedule remains the baseline and control journey.

The adaptive system can propose:

- Keep existing contact.
- Replace with approved contextual template.
- Reschedule within permitted windows.
- Defer.
- Suppress.

Never blindly add follow-up messages simply because a consumer replied.

Before any proactive send, re-read authoritative eligibility, permissions, payment/plan state, timezone and contact history.

Preserve the supplied rolling contact limits:

- One attempt per rolling 24 hours.
- Three attempts per rolling seven days.
- Monday to Friday, 9 am inclusive to 6 pm exclusive, in verified local time.
- Cross-channel limits.
- Account holds and consumer opt-outs.

Preserve the existing regression scenarios, particularly delayed opt-outs, pending payments, unknown timezones, active plans, hardship holds and duplicate events.

The conversational interface must not bypass the existing safety engine.
